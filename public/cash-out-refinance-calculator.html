How a Cash-Out Refinance Works

A cash-out refinance replaces your existing mortgage with a new, larger loan. The difference between your old loan balance and the new loan amount is paid to you in cash at closing.

Simple Example

Say your home is worth $500,000 and you owe $280,000 on your mortgage. You want to pull out $80,000 for a home renovation.

Your new loan would be $360,000 ($280,000 existing balance + $80,000 cash). At closing you receive $80,000 minus closing costs. Your old mortgage is paid off and you now have one new loan at the new rate and term.

Key Point Unlike a HELOC or home equity loan, a cash-out refi replaces your entire mortgage — not just adds a second lien. This means your rate and term reset on the whole balance, not just the cash portion.

How Much Can You Pull Out? — By Loan Type

The amount you can access depends on your Loan-to-Value (LTV) ratio after the refinance — and that ceiling varies significantly by loan type. Here's a complete breakdown:

Conventional Cash-Out Refinance

The most common option for primary residences and second homes. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac cap conventional cash-out refis at 80% LTV on primary residences. On a $500,000 home owing $280,000, your max new loan is $400,000 — giving you up to $120,000 cash before closing costs.

FHA Cash-Out Refinance

FHA also caps at 80% LTV, same as conventional. The difference: FHA allows lower credit scores (minimum 500, though most lenders want 580+) and is more flexible with DTI. The catch — FHA adds mortgage insurance premium (MIP) that you pay for the life of the loan. If you have good credit, conventional is almost always better. FHA cash-out makes sense if your credit score is in the 580–639 range.

VA Cash-Out Refinance

This is where veterans have a massive advantage. VA cash-out refis allow up to 100% LTV — meaning you can pull out virtually all of your equity. On a $500,000 home owing $280,000, eligible veterans could access up to $220,000 in equity minus the VA funding fee (2.15% for first use, typically rolled in). No PMI ever. This is one of the most powerful financial tools available to veterans and active service members.

Jumbo Cash-Out Refinance

For loan amounts above the conforming limit ($806,500 in most areas for 2026), jumbo lenders typically cap cash-out at 70–75% LTV — more conservative than conventional. Requirements are stricter too: most jumbo lenders want 700+ credit score, 12+ months of reserves, and full income documentation. Rates run 0.25–0.5% above conventional.

Investment Property Cash-Out

Pulling equity from a rental property is possible but comes with tighter limits. Conventional guidelines allow cash-out on investment properties up to 75% LTV for single-family, and 70% LTV for 2–4 unit properties. You'll also need 6 months of reserves for each financed property you own. Rates are typically 0.75–1.5% higher than primary residence rates.

DSCR Cash-Out (Investment Properties)

For investors who don't want to show personal income, DSCR cash-out refis are available up to 75–80% LTV depending on the lender. No income verification required — the property's rental income does the qualifying. Rates run higher than conventional (typically 7–8.5% in 2026) but the flexibility is unmatched for investors with complex tax situations.

Non-QM / Bank Statement Cash-Out

Self-employed borrowers who can't qualify using traditional income documentation can use bank statement loans for cash-out. Most bank statement programs allow up to 80% LTV on primary, 75% on investment. Rates are higher — expect 7.5–9% — but it opens the door for borrowers who show low income on tax returns due to business write-offs.

⚠️ Texas Cash-Out Special Rules Texas has unique constitutional protections on home equity. Texas cash-out refis (called Texas 50(a)(6) loans) are capped at 80% LTV, require a 12-day cooling-off period after application, can only be done once per year, and carry additional disclosures. If your property is in Texas, make sure your lender is familiar with Texas home equity law.

The Rate Question: Does It Make Sense?

The biggest factor in whether a cash-out refi makes financial sense is how your current mortgage rate compares to today's rates.

If Your Current Rate Is Higher Than Today's Rate

This is the ideal scenario. You get cash out AND lower your rate at the same time. Your monthly payment may actually drop even with a larger loan balance. This is a win on multiple levels.

If Your Current Rate Is Lower Than Today's Rate

This is where it gets complicated. If you locked in a 3% rate in 2021 and today's rates are 6.69%, a cash-out refi means refinancing your entire balance at a much higher rate. Your payment will jump significantly — not just on the cash portion, but on every dollar you owe.

⚠️ The Rate Trap If you have a low existing rate, consider a HELOC or home equity loan instead. These add a second payment but leave your existing low-rate mortgage untouched. Our equity calculator lets you compare all three options side by side.

Closing Costs: What to Expect

Cash-out refis come with closing costs — typically 2–3% of the new loan amount. On a $400,000 new loan that's $8,000–$12,000.

You have two options:

  • Pay closing costs upfront — lower loan balance, better long-term math
  • Roll closing costs into the loan — nothing due at closing, but you pay interest on the costs over the life of the loan

Our calculator handles both scenarios — toggle between "Pay upfront" and "Roll into loan" to see the difference.

Break-Even: How Long Until the Cash Pays Off?

If your payment goes up because you're refinancing from a lower rate, you need to calculate how long it takes for the cash you received to "pay for" the higher monthly cost. This is the break-even point.

Example: Your payment goes up $400/month. You pulled out $60,000 in cash. Break-even = $60,000 ÷ $400 = 150 months (12.5 years). If you plan to stay in the home longer than 12.5 years, the math works. If not, a HELOC might be smarter.

Current Cash-Out Refi Rates (May 2026)

Cash-out refinance rates typically run 0.25–0.5% higher than standard rate-and-term refi rates because lenders see them as slightly higher risk. As of May 2026, expect rates in the 6.5–7.25% range for well-qualified borrowers on conventional cash-out refis.

Your actual rate depends on your credit score, LTV, loan amount, and the lender. Getting quotes from at least 3 lenders is always worth it — rate differences of 0.25% on a $400,000 loan equal about $60/month and over $21,000 over 30 years.
