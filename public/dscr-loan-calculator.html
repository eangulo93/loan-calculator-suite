DSCR Loan Calculator: How to Qualify Based on Rental Income (2026)

Updated May 2026 · 6 min read · myloanbreakdown.com

If you're a real estate investor tired of handing over two years of tax returns, W-2s, and pay stubs just to buy a rental property — a DSCR loan might be exactly what you've been looking for. DSCR loans qualify you based on your property's rental income, not your personal income. No job history required. No personal income verification. Just numbers.

This guide explains exactly how DSCR loans work, how lenders calculate your ratio, and what you need to qualify in 2026.

What Is DSCR?

DSCR stands for Debt Service Coverage Ratio. It's a simple formula lenders use to measure whether a rental property generates enough income to cover its loan payments:

DSCR Formula DSCR = Gross Monthly Rent ÷ Monthly PITIA (Principal + Interest + Taxes + Insurance + HOA)

A DSCR of 1.0 means the property breaks even — rent exactly covers the payment. A DSCR above 1.0 means the property generates positive cash flow. Below 1.0 means you'd need to cover the gap out of pocket each month.

Quick Example

Say you're buying a rental property with a $1,850/month PITIA payment and it rents for $2,400/month:

DSCR = $2,400 ÷ $1,850 = 1.30

A 1.30 DSCR qualifies at most lenders and gets you their best rate pricing.

DSCR Loan Requirements in 2026

While every lender is different, here's what most DSCR lenders require:

Credit Score

Most DSCR lenders require a minimum 640 credit score. For the best rates — typically available at 6.125% or below as of May 2026 — you'll want a 740+ score with 75% LTV or better.

Down Payment

Expect to put down 20–25% on a DSCR loan. Some lenders allow 15% with strong credit and high DSCR, but 20–25% is standard. Unlike conventional loans, going below 20% doesn't trigger PMI — it just isn't offered.

Property Types

DSCR loans work on single-family rentals, 2–4 unit properties, condos, and in some cases short-term rentals (Airbnb, VRBO). Most lenders require the property to be non-owner-occupied — you cannot use a DSCR loan on your primary residence.

No Income Documentation

This is the key differentiator. DSCR lenders do not verify your personal income, employment history, or debt-to-income ratio. They look at the property's numbers, not yours. Self-employed investors, 1099 earners, and business owners who show low personal income on paper can qualify where conventional loans would reject them.

✅ Who DSCR Loans Are Best For Real estate investors who own multiple properties, self-employed borrowers with complex tax situations, high earners who write off significant expenses, and anyone building a rental portfolio who wants to keep personal finances separate from investment activity.
⚠️ Who Should Think Twice First-time investors buying a property that barely breaks even. If your DSCR is below 1.0, you're taking on a property that costs you money every month before maintenance, repairs, or vacancy. Run the numbers carefully before committing.

How to Improve Your DSCR

If your DSCR is coming in too low to qualify, you have three levers:

1. Increase the Rent

Get a market rent analysis from a local property manager. If the current asking rent is below market, a higher rent estimate (supported by a 1007 rent schedule from your appraiser) could push your DSCR above the threshold.

2. Increase the Down Payment

A larger down payment means a smaller loan, which means a lower monthly PITIA. Going from 20% to 25% down can meaningfully improve your DSCR when the numbers are tight.

3. Negotiate the Purchase Price

Lower purchase price → lower loan → lower payment → better DSCR. If a property is borderline, sometimes a modest price reduction from the seller gets you over the threshold.

Bottom Line

DSCR loans are one of the most powerful tools available to real estate investors in 2026 — especially those who are self-employed or own multiple properties. The tradeoff is a higher interest rate and larger down payment compared to conventional financing. But for investors who value speed, simplicity, and the ability to scale without income documentation hurdles, that tradeoff is almost always worth it.
