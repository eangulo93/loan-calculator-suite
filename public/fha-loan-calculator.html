FHA Loan Calculator: Low Down Payment Guide (2026)

Updated May 2026 · 8 min read · myloanbreakdown.com

FHA loans are the go-to option for first-time homebuyers and anyone with less-than-perfect credit. With just 3.5% down and credit scores as low as 580, FHA opens the door to homeownership for millions of Americans who can't qualify for conventional financing. But FHA comes with costs that many buyers don't fully understand until closing day — primarily mortgage insurance that lasts the life of the loan.

This guide covers everything: how to calculate your FHA payment, what mortgage insurance actually costs, the loan limits for 2026, and — critically — when FHA makes sense and when you'd be better off with a conventional loan.

FHA Loan Basics: What Makes It Different

FHA loans are insured by the Federal Housing Administration — a division of HUD. Because the government backs the loan, lenders can approve borrowers with lower credit scores and smaller down payments than conventional guidelines allow. In exchange, borrowers pay mortgage insurance premiums (MIP) that fund the FHA insurance program.

Key FHA Advantages

  • 3.5% minimum down payment at 580+ credit score
  • 10% down option for credit scores 500–579
  • Higher DTI allowed — up to 57% back-end DTI with compensating factors
  • Gift funds allowed for the entire down payment
  • More flexible on recent credit events (bankruptcy, foreclosure)
  • Assumable — a buyer can take over your FHA loan when you sell

FHA Mortgage Insurance: The Full Cost

This is where FHA buyers are often surprised. FHA requires two types of mortgage insurance:

Upfront MIP (UFMIP)

1.75% of the base loan amount, charged at closing. Almost always rolled into the loan — so you don't pay it out of pocket, but you do pay interest on it for the life of the loan.

Annual MIP

Paid monthly as part of your mortgage payment. The rate depends on your loan term, loan amount, and LTV:

Loan TermLTVAnnual MIP RateDuration
30-year>90% (less than 10% down)0.55%Life of loan
30-year≤90% (10%+ down)0.50%11 years

⚠️ The Lifetime MIP Problem If you put less than 10% down on an FHA loan, the annual MIP never goes away — unlike conventional PMI which cancels automatically at 80% LTV. On a 30-year loan, that's 360 payments of mortgage insurance. This is the single biggest reason to choose conventional over FHA when you qualify for both.

HUD allows lenders to go down to 500, but most lenders set their own overlays — minimums of 580, 600, or even 620. If your score is below 580, expect to shop harder for a willing lender and put 10% down.

FHA DTI Limits: More Flexible Than You Think

FHA is significantly more flexible on debt-to-income ratio than conventional loans:

  • Standard: 31% front-end DTI (housing only) / 43% back-end DTI (all debts)
  • With compensating factors: Up to 57% back-end DTI (HUD 4000.1)
  • Compensating factors include: Residual income, large down payment, minimal payment shock, significant reserves

A borrower with a 55% DTI, 620 credit score, and strong compensating factors can get FHA approval — something no conventional lender would touch.

The FHA MIP Escape: Refinancing Out

If you took an FHA loan with less than 10% down, you're stuck with MIP for the life of the loan — unless you refinance into a conventional loan once you have enough equity.

The magic number: once your home's value gives you 20% equity, you can refinance to conventional with no PMI. If home values have appreciated since you bought, this can happen faster than you'd expect.

Example: You bought at $400,000 with 3.5% down in 2023. Home is now worth $460,000. Your loan balance is ~$375,000. LTV = 375 ÷ 460 = 81.5%. Still slightly above 80%, but close. A few more months of payments or a small amount of appreciation gets you to 80% — then you refinance to conventional, MIP disappears, and you save $150–$200/month permanently.

✅ FHA Strategy: Buy Now, Refinance Later Many buyers use FHA to get into a home sooner with lower down payment requirements, then refinance to conventional once they've built 20% equity. It's a legitimate strategy — you pay MIP for a few years in exchange for homeownership years earlier than if you waited to save 20% down.

FHA Streamline Refinance

Already have an FHA loan? The FHA Streamline Refi lets you refinance to a lower rate with minimal documentation — no appraisal required, reduced income verification, and faster processing. You must have made at least 6 payments on your current FHA loan and there must be a tangible benefit (lower rate or shorter term).

The new MIP rates apply to the streamlined loan — so if MIP rates have dropped since you got your original FHA loan, a streamline refi can lower both your rate and your MIP simultaneously.

FHA Property Requirements

FHA has minimum property standards that conventional loans don't. The FHA appraiser checks that the property is safe, sound, and secure. Common FHA issues that can derail a deal:

  • Peeling paint (especially on homes built before 1978 — lead paint concern)
  • Missing handrails on stairs
  • Roof with less than 2 years of useful life remaining
  • Cracked or broken windows
  • No permanent heat source
  • Evidence of structural issues or water damage

In competitive markets, some sellers prefer conventional offers over FHA because of these stricter property requirements. If this is a concern, consider whether you could qualify for conventional instead.
