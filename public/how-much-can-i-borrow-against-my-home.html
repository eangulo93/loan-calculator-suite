How Much Can I Borrow Against My Home? (2026 Guide)

Updated May 2026 · 7 min read · myloanbreakdown.com

If you've owned your home for a few years, there's a good chance you've built up significant equity — and that equity is one of the most powerful financial tools available to you. But how much can you actually borrow against it? The answer depends on your home's value, your current mortgage balance, your credit score, and which loan product you choose.

This guide walks through exactly how lenders calculate your maximum borrowing amount, what the LTV limits are for each product, and how to figure out your number before ever talking to a lender.

The Formula: How Lenders Calculate Your Maximum

Lenders use a metric called Combined Loan-to-Value (CLTV) to determine how much you can borrow. The formula is:

Maximum Borrowing Amount Formula Max New Loan = (Home Value × Max LTV%) − Current Mortgage Balance

Real Example

📊 Sample Calculation

Home Value$450,000
× 80% LTV limit$360,000
− Current Mortgage Balance$245,000
= Maximum You Can Borrow$115,000

On a $450,000 home with a $245,000 mortgage balance, at 80% LTV you could access up to $115,000 in equity. Our calculator does this math instantly — just enter your numbers.

HELOC: Flexible Access Up to 85% CLTV

A Home Equity Line of Credit (HELOC) works like a credit card secured by your home. Most lenders allow up to 85% CLTV, which is higher than the 80% cap on cash-out refis. The rate is variable — tied to the Prime Rate — and you only pay interest on what you draw.

Using our example above ($450,000 home, $245,000 mortgage), at 85% CLTV you could access:

$450,000 × 85% − $245,000 = $137,500

⚠️ HELOC Rate Risk HELOCs are variable rate. As of May 2026, the Prime Rate is 8.50% and most HELOCs are priced at Prime + 0.50% or higher. If the Fed raises rates, your HELOC payment goes up automatically. Factor this into your budget.

Home Equity Loan: Fixed Rate Up to 85% CLTV

A home equity loan gives you a lump sum at a fixed rate — your payment never changes. Same 85% CLTV limit as a HELOC, but the structure is completely different. You get the money upfront, pay it back in equal installments, and the rate is locked for the life of the loan.

Current home equity loan rates as of May 2026: approximately 8.35% for well-qualified borrowers. Best for one-time large expenses where you know exactly how much you need.

What Affects How Much You Can Borrow?

The LTV limit is just the ceiling. Your actual approved amount depends on several other factors:

Credit Score

Most lenders require a minimum 620 credit score for home equity products. But your score also affects how close to the LTV ceiling they'll let you go:

Debt-to-Income Ratio (DTI)

Even if you have 85% CLTV available, lenders won't approve more than your income can support. Most home equity lenders cap total DTI at 43% — meaning all your monthly debt payments (mortgage, new loan, car, credit cards) can't exceed 43% of your gross monthly income.

Home Value Verification

Lenders order their own appraisal — they don't use Zillow or Redfin. If your home appraises lower than you expected, your maximum borrowing amount drops accordingly. Before applying, get a realistic estimate: look at recent comparable sales in your neighborhood, not just your Zestimate.

Property Type

Single-family primary residences get the best terms. Investment properties and second homes face tighter LTV limits — typically 70–75% maximum CLTV, and rates run 0.5–1.0% higher.

If You Own Your Home Free and Clear

No mortgage? You're in an even stronger position. With no existing lien, lenders can give you access to a larger portion of your home's value. Most lenders will go up to 80% LTV on a free and clear property, and some will go to 85% for well-qualified borrowers.

On a $450,000 free and clear home, that means up to $360,000–$382,500 available depending on the lender and your credit profile. You can access this through a first-position home equity loan, HELOC, or a traditional mortgage.

✅ Free & Clear Advantage Owning free and clear makes you a lower-risk borrower in lenders' eyes — no competing lien means they have first position on the collateral. You may qualify for lower rates and higher approval amounts than borrowers with existing mortgages.

How to Calculate Your Number Before Talking to a Lender

You should know your maximum before any lender conversation. Here's how to do it yourself in three steps:

Step 1 — Get your home's value: Check Zillow, Redfin, and Realtor.com. Average the estimates, then discount slightly since lenders' appraisals often come in conservative. Or pay $400–$500 for a certified appraisal if you want a precise number.

Step 2 — Find your mortgage balance: Log into your mortgage servicer's website or check your latest statement. Use the current payoff balance, not the original loan amount.

Step 3 — Run the formula: (Home Value × 0.80) − Mortgage Balance = Max Cash-Out available at 80% LTV. Multiply by 0.85 for the HELOC/HE Loan ceiling.
