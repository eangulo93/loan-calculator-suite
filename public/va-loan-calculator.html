The Most Powerful Mortgage Benefit in America

VA loans allow eligible veterans, active-duty service members, and surviving spouses to buy a home with zero down payment, no PMI, and the lowest average rates of any loan type. If you've earned this benefit through service, you should understand exactly how to use it.

The VA home loan benefit is one of the most valuable — and most underused — financial benefits available to veterans. Hundreds of thousands of veterans who qualify never use it, often because they don't know they're eligible or they don't fully understand how the loan works. This guide fixes that.

VA Loan Benefits: What Makes This Different

💰

Zero Down Payment

Buy a home with no down payment — preserving your savings for emergencies, investments, or life.

🚫

No PMI — Ever

No private mortgage insurance regardless of your down payment. This saves $100–$300/month vs. conventional with less than 20% down.

📉

Lowest Rates Available

VA loans average 0.5–0.75% below conventional rates due to the government guarantee. On a $400K loan that's $100–$150/month.

🔄

Reusable Benefit

You can use your VA benefit multiple times throughout your life — including while still paying off a previous VA loan.

🏠

100% Cash-Out Refi

VA cash-out refinancing allows you to access up to 100% of your home's value — far beyond the 80% cap on conventional loans.

🛡️

Assumable Loan

VA loans can be assumed by a buyer when you sell — a major selling advantage if your rate is lower than future market rates.

Who Is Eligible for a VA Loan?

Eligibility is based on your service history. Generally you qualify if you meet one of the following:

Service CategoryMinimum Service Requirement
Active duty (wartime)90 consecutive days
Active duty (peacetime)181 consecutive days
National Guard / Reserves6 years, or 90 days active duty under Title 10
Discharged for service-connected disabilityAny length of service
Surviving spouseSpouse of veteran who died in service or from service-connected disability

The first step is obtaining your Certificate of Eligibility (COE) from the VA. Your lender can often pull this directly through the VA's online system in minutes — you don't need to request it yourself first.

VA Loan Limits in 2026

In 2020, Congress eliminated VA loan limits for veterans with full entitlement — meaning if you have never used your VA benefit (or have paid off and restored it), there is no maximum loan amount. The VA will guarantee any loan amount your lender approves you for with zero down.

If you have remaining entitlement (you have an active VA loan you haven't paid off), county loan limits apply to the zero-down portion. The 2026 conforming loan limit is $806,500 in most areas, with higher limits in high-cost counties (up to $1,209,750).

✅ Full Entitlement = No Loan Limit Most first-time VA buyers have full entitlement. If you've never used your VA benefit or have fully paid off and restored a previous VA loan, you can borrow any amount your lender approves — $500K, $800K, $1.2M — with zero down and no PMI.

The VA Funding Fee: What It Is and What You Pay

The VA doesn't charge monthly mortgage insurance, but it does charge a one-time VA Funding Fee at closing. This fee funds the VA loan program so it remains available for future veterans. It's typically rolled into the loan — so you don't pay it out of pocket.

VA Loan Qualification Requirements

Credit Score

The VA itself has no minimum credit score requirement. However, lenders set their own minimums — typically 580–620 for most lenders, with 640+ recommended for competitive rates. Some VA-specialist lenders will go to 500 with strong compensating factors.

Debt-to-Income Ratio

The VA uses a guideline of 41% back-end DTI, but this is not a hard cap. The VA's residual income test is what really matters — you must have enough income left after all monthly payments to cover basic living expenses for your family size and geographic region.

Residual Income

This is the VA's unique qualifying factor. After all monthly obligations, you must have a minimum amount of money left over each month based on family size and region. This test is often more forgiving than a strict DTI cap — a veteran with a 50% DTI but strong income can still qualify if residual income requirements are met.

Property Requirements

The property must be your primary residence — VA loans cannot be used for investment properties or vacation homes. The home must also meet VA minimum property requirements (MPRs) — basic safety and livability standards that the VA appraiser will verify.

VA IRRRL: The Streamline Refinance

If you already have a VA loan, the Interest Rate Reduction Refinance Loan (IRRRL) — also called the VA Streamline Refi — is the simplest refinance available. No appraisal required, minimal paperwork, no income verification in most cases. You just need to refinance into a lower rate than your current VA loan.

The funding fee is just 0.50% — the lowest of any VA loan type. If you locked a VA loan at a higher rate in the last few years, the IRRRL is how you get the current lower rate with minimal friction.

VA Cash-Out Refinance: Access 100% of Your Equity

The VA cash-out refi is unlike any other loan product available. You can refinance up to 100% of your home's appraised value — compared to 80% for conventional cash-out and 80% for FHA. If your home has appreciated significantly, this is an enormous advantage.

You can also use the VA cash-out refi to convert a conventional or FHA loan into a VA loan — picking up the lower rate and eliminating PMI in one transaction.
